How to read RNAwiki

#Evidence stars

Stars rate human evidence for a compound overall — not for your particular goal, and not how well it will work for you. Animal-only evidence is capped at two stars and labelled.

  • ★★★★★ — consistent, replicated human trials
  • ★★★★☆ — good human evidence, some inconsistency
  • ★★★☆☆ — mixed or limited human trials
  • ★★☆☆☆ — early, small, or animal-only evidence
  • ★☆☆☆☆ — mechanistic or anecdotal only

#Regulator status — the colours

A colour says what the FDA, or the relevant regulator for that molecule, currently calls it. It is not a statement about where you can buy it, and it is not anyone here's opinion. A medicine can be approved and still be prescription-only.

  • 🟢 — FDA Approved
  • 🟡 — Regulated as a supplement
  • 🔵 — Prescription-only medicine
  • 🟠 — Off-Label
  • 🔴 — Not Approved
  • ⚫ — Controlled

#How you get it — the separate question

Availability is shown separately from approval, as its own statement on every compound: no prescription needed, over the counter, pharmacy medicine, prescription only, controlled, or not approved for human use. The classification shown is Singapore's, because that is the one regulator I track in full — it is a good guide almost everywhere, but check your own country's rules before assuming. Where a compound is prescription-only the page says so and gives no dose.

#Who decides any of this

No clinician has reviewed these pages. Nothing here is a professional's opinion, an endorsement, or medical advice, and no colour or star should be read as one. The stars are an editorial rating of the published human evidence, applied by one consistent rule across every compound. The colours are not a judgement at all — they are a regulator's current call on that molecule, recorded rather than formed here. Where the two disagree they are shown disagreeing: a compound can be approved and still have weak evidence for what you want it for, and the reverse.
