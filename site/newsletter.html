What lands in your inbox

What actually changed this week — and whether it touches you
A drug gets approved. A supplement gets pulled. A big trial reads out and every headline mangles it. Most of it will not affect you. I'll tell you the one that does, in plain English, and what — if anything — to do differently.
The claim on the label, held against the trial behind it
Usually the number is real and the sentence wrapped around it is not. A supplement that says it "improves emotional wellbeing" turns out to have measured one depression score. A weight-loss figure quoted as a 14-week result was actually 52 weeks, most of it unblinded. Once you can see that move, you cannot unsee it — and you stop paying for it.
The question worth asking before you agree to anything
Not refuse it — ask about it. What is this for, what does it actually do, what happens if I don't take it, and is there something cheaper that does the same job? Ten minutes of understanding is worth more than any second opinion you'll pay for.
