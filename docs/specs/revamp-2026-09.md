# rnawiki revamp, September 2026

## Mission

rnawiki.com is a drug and longevity knowledge graph for biohackers and self-experimenters, newcomer through expert, anchored in Singapore. The corpus-20k run (FINAL REPORT in `docs/worklogs/corpus-20k.md`, PR #18) rebuilt the site to 28,832 pages in three tiers and cut page-to-page overlap from 0.80 to 0.28. It left the following open, and this run closes all of it:

1. The indexing ruler is miscalibrated. The 11-of-15 threshold likely counts fields that are blank on every page and fields that cannot apply to some tiers. Tier 2 (Clinical) passes 26 of 4,477 pages against Tier 1's 610 of 1,719, a sixtyfold gap that is almost certainly a schema artefact, not a data fact.
2. The report claims DrugBank holds data "nothing free supplied." That claim is false. NCATS Inxight Drugs (public domain, curated MoA/targets/uses, and a 2025 curated drug-interaction dataset), DrugCentral (CC BY-SA, indications, contraindications, MoA, FDA/EMA/PMDA approvals), DailyMed/openFDA labels (public domain), PubChem, UniProt, GSRS, the FDA Orange Book, and HSA's own registered-products listing on data.gov.sg (Open Data Licence, commercial use permitted) together cover every gap named. No DrugBank licence is to be purchased or assumed.
3. Regulatory status for SG, UK, AU, JP is blank on every page and patent status has no source. All of these are free public registers that were simply not wired in.
4. 966 identity items (214 name collisions, 697 suspected merges, 55 held conflicts) await a human, and the rendered duplicate check required by `docs/specs/identity-resolution.md` never ran.
5. Tier 3 (22,636 pages) is three quarters stubs and the report asserts only real-world events can improve them. Several genuinely unique sections can be computed per compound from data already held.
6. The RSC payload is 59–64% of every document; live text-to-HTML is 3.3–5.5%.
7. The corpus is not in any versioned remote. Two of its three copies die with the machine.
8. All success metrics are proxies. No Search Console signal has been captured.

## Definition of done

State file `data/revamp/state.json` shows gates G0 through G7 as `PASSED` or `BLOCKED-WITH-EVIDENCE` (the latter only for items that require Felix's credentials or account access, each with the exact error or missing input named). The site is deployed on Railway with all three tiers verified live, redirects verified, sitemap and IndexNow updated, CI green with the new checks, and `docs/worklogs/revamp-2026-09.md` carries a FINAL REPORT 2 section with every before/after table, every source with its licence, every decision left for Felix stated with numbers, and every skipped item with its reason.

## Operating rules

These are not suggestions. Violating one is a failed step.

1. Read before acting. Phase 0 reads the corpus-20k FINAL REPORT, the identity spec, the worked examples, and the schema code before anything is changed. Do not re-derive what is already recorded there; cite it.
2. No stubs, no deferrals. The phrases "in a future iteration", "could be extended", "left as a follow-up", "for now", "simplified version", "TODO", "placeholder", "mock" are forbidden in code, worklog and report. If you catch yourself writing one, stop and do the thing. If it genuinely cannot be done, the only acceptable output is a BLOCKERS entry naming the exact command that failed, its full error, three retry attempts with backoff, the alternative source tried, and what would unblock it.
3. Every claim is backed by an artefact. "Verified" without a command output path is not verified. Every worklog entry has a timestamp, the command or script, and the path of what it produced. Numbers in the report link to the CSV or JSON they came from.
4. Same ruler before and after. Every measurement reuses the corpus-20k scripts and samples (the 803 draw, the 324 draw, the 604 medicines, the eight live samples) so the deltas are real. New measurements get their own recorded method.
5. Never lower a threshold to pass a gate. If a gate fails, fix the cause or record the failure. The one exception is Phase 1, whose whole purpose is to re-derive thresholds by the same measurement method that produced them.
6. Corpus-scale work runs outside the context window. Scripts under `scripts/revamp/` read the corpus, write results to `data/revamp/`, and print at most a 50-row summary. Never iterate over pages inside the conversation. Never paste more than 50 rows of anything into context. Subagent reports are summarised to 20 lines or fewer in the worklog.
7. Invariants that must hold at every commit: `robots.txt` untouched; Tier 3 carries `noindex` and is absent from every sitemap; all 870 legacy redirects answer 308; zero orphans across all legacy slugs including any created by this run; frozen search bar identical to 0.00 px at the three test widths.
8. Licences are tracked per source in `docs/data/LICENSES.md` with: source, URL, retrieval date, licence, whether attribution is required, whether share-alike applies, whether redistribution is permitted, whether commercial use is permitted. Data under a non-commercial licence is never joined into rendered pages or into the release candidate. If a source's terms cannot be found or forbid bulk access, record it and do not scrape it.
9. The reader is a biohacker making real decisions. Predicted interactions are labelled predicted, every time, with the derivation shown. The absence of a found interaction renders as "no interaction found in [sources checked, date]", never as "safe" or "no interaction". Unknowns render as "not found in [register] as of [date]", never as blanks. For substances that are controlled under any scheduled list ingested (SG Misuse of Drugs Act, US DEA schedules, AU SUSMP Schedules 8–9, UN conventions), pages carry regulatory status, pharmacology, mechanism and interaction evidence, and never dosing, timing, administration or combination protocols. These rendering rules are unit-tested in Phase 4.
10. Slop, as Felix defines it: a passage where consecutive claims do not logically follow from each other, even if the prose sounds good. Also: template sentences with a name swapped in, filler, and any label naming the narrative device ("the problem", "the lesson"). Derived text is generated from stored fields and computed values, never free-written, and the slop gate in Phase 4 samples rendered pages until two consecutive draws come back clean. Fix the generator, never the individual page.
11. Do not wait on Felix mid-run. Where a judgment call is his (residual band, hub expansion beyond the first batch), make the defensible default that the measurements support, mark it reversible in the report, and continue. Where an action is his (credentials, account uploads), record the blocker and continue with everything that does not depend on it.
12. Parallelise what is independent. Source ingestion in Phase 2 runs as parallel subagents, one per source. Identity matching and rendered-duplicate checking in Phase 3 can run concurrently. Do not serialise work that has no dependency.
13. Commit at every gate and at every step that produces a file over 1 MB or takes over 10 minutes. Branch: `revamp/2026-09`. PRs merge on green CI.

## Session protocol

On start: read this spec, `data/revamp/state.json`, and the last 60 lines of the worklog. State the phase and step being resumed and the evidence that the previous step completed. Then continue.

## Phase 1 — Correct the ruler
Lead: Fable 5.1.

1.1 Denominator. Fields flagged `universal_blank` in 0.2 leave the denominator until a Phase 2 source fills them. Track them as `pending_source`, not `absent`. Recompute presence percentages and record the change.

1.2 Applicability matrix. `docs/specs/field-applicability.md`: a table of the 15 fields against the three tiers and the LONGEVITY/CLINICAL/DEVELOPMENT models, marking each cell applicable or not-applicable with a one-line reason. A withdrawal reason applies only to withdrawn arcs. An ITP entry or ageing-trial field applies only to LONGEVITY. A max-phase field applies everywhere. Where a field's applicability is unclear, look at its presence distribution: a field present on 40% of Tier 1 and 0.2% of Tier 2 is a structural non-applicability, not a data gap. Presence is henceforth counted over applicable fields only.

1.3 Re-derive the threshold per tier using exactly the method quoted in 0.1, applied within each tier over applicable fields. Record the method in words and as a script `scripts/revamp/derive_threshold.py`, the resulting threshold per tier, and the positional and lexical overlap of the resulting indexable set per tier. No hand-chosen numbers. If the method produces a threshold that admits pages measuring over 0.20 positional or over the lexical null of 0.353, record that and raise the threshold by the smallest step that clears both lines, stating the step.

1.4 Explain Tier 2. `data/revamp/tier2-anomaly.csv`: for each of the 15 fields, presence in Tier 2 before and after the applicability correction. The worklog states, with numbers, whether Tier 2's 26 of 4,477 was a schema artefact (corrected pass count is materially higher) or a data fact (clinical pages genuinely lack X, Y, Z that a Phase 2 source may fill). Both can be true; say which fields fall in which bucket.

1.5 Human-readable sanity. Sample 20 pages per tier at threshold, threshold+1 and threshold−1 (180 pages). `scripts/revamp/threshold_sanity.py` writes their titles, field counts and rendered first 300 words to `data/revamp/threshold-sanity.md`. Read it. Write three sentences per tier on whether pages at the line read as useful to a biohacker. Flag the file for Felix. Proceed regardless.

1.6 Re-evaluate Gates 1, 1b and 2 from corpus-20k under the corrected ruler. Record pass/fail and the deltas.

**Gate G1**: per-tier thresholds documented with derivation script; Tier 2 anomaly explained with numbers; new indexable set computed and counted per tier; original gates re-evaluated.

## Phase 2 — Wire the open sources
Subagents on Opus 5 (`source-ingester`), one per source, in parallel. Lead integrates and evaluates the gate.

Each subagent, for its source: retrieve (bulk download preferred over API; API preferred over page fetching; never scrape against terms) → store raw under `data/sources/<source>/<date>/` with `manifest.json` → parse → map to corpus pages using the mapping rules below → write `data/sources/<source>/mapped.parquet` → write a coverage report `data/sources/<source>/coverage.json` (pages matched per tier, fields gained per tier, unmatched records count) → append the LICENSES entry → summarise in 20 lines to the lead. Retries: three with exponential backoff; then an alternative endpoint or mirror; then a BLOCKERS entry with the full error. Rate limits: respect them; register for API keys where offered.

Mapping rules, in priority order:
(a) UNII exact match.
(b) Full InChIKey exact match.
(c) InChIKey skeleton (first 14 characters) match → link as `form_of` (salt, ester, hydrate, stereoisomer), never merge.
(d) Normalised name (lowercase, salt and counter-ion suffixes stripped from a maintained list in `scripts/revamp/salts.txt`, stereo prefixes stripped) → candidate only; requires (a) or (b) confirmation, else goes to the Phase 3 review list.
Multi-ingredient products map to each component page and to the combination page where one exists.

Sources and what to take:

1. **NCATS Inxight Drugs** (drugs.ncats.io; public domain). Substance records, mechanism of action, targets, approved and off-label uses, marketing status by jurisdiction, and the curated pharmacokinetics, adverse events and drug-drug interaction dataset released on its Downloads page in 2025. This closes the interactions field and the mechanism gap on the 3,971 pages without a ChEMBL id. Use the API and the bulk downloads.
2. **GSRS / FDA Global Substance Registration System** (public domain). UNII, preferred name, synonyms, substance class, structure where present, for every substance that has appeared in any US product including supplements and botanicals. This is the identity spine for Phase 3.
3. **PubChem** (public domain, PUG REST). CID, SMILES, InChI, InChIKey, MW, cLogP, TPSA, for every page with a resolvable name or structure. Batch by InChIKey where held, else by name.
4. **openFDA drug label / DailyMed SPL** (public domain; bulk JSON downloads at openFDA). Take the pre-split fields: `indications_and_usage`, `contraindications`, `warnings_and_cautions`, `drug_interactions`, `use_in_specific_populations`, `overdosage`, `mechanism_of_action`, `pharmacokinetics`, `clinical_pharmacology`, `boxed_warning`, plus `openfda.unii`, `openfda.rxcui`, `set_id`, `effective_time`. From `pharmacokinetics` and `drug_interactions`, extract CYP enzymes and transporters with the drug's role (substrate, inhibitor, inducer) and strength where stated (strong, moderate, weak) → `cyp_profile` field. Keep the label version date.
5. **DrugCentral** (drugcentral.org; CC BY-SA 4.0; PostgreSQL dump). Indications and contraindications (`omop_relationship`), MoA targets and action types (`act_table_full`), pharmacologic action, approvals by FDA/EMA/PMDA with dates, FAERS-derived adverse events. The PMDA flag closes Japan.
6. **IUPHAR/BPS Guide to Pharmacology** (CC BY-SA 4.0; downloads). Ligand-target pairs with action type and affinity, especially for biologics and receptor pharmacology.
7. **UniProt** (CC BY 4.0). Sequences for biologic drugs; target protein records for all targets.
8. **ChEMBL** (CC BY-SA 3.0). Already used; confirm the run holds `activities` with pChEMBL, `drug_mechanism`, `drug_indication`, `max_phase`, `withdrawn_flag/year/reason`, `molecule_hierarchy` (parent/salt). Fill what is missing.
9. **FDA Orange Book** (public domain; downloadable data files) and **Purple Book** (public domain). Patents with expiry, exclusivity with expiry, RLD flag, TE codes, for approved products; biologic exclusivity from the Purple Book. This is the patent field.
10. **WITHDRAWN** (Charité; check licence on site and record). Withdrawn drugs with reason, country, year. Cross-check with ChEMBL `withdrawn_*` and Inxight status.
11. **HSA Singapore, Listing of Registered Therapeutic Products** (data.gov.sg dataset `d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084`; Open Data Licence, free for commercial use; API). Product name, active ingredient(s), registrant, forensic classification (POM, P, GSL), licence status. Register for a data.gov.sg API key. Match on active ingredient, not brand. Then search data.gov.sg for the MOH Standard Drug List / Medication Assistance Fund list and ingest subsidy status if published. Then pull the First Schedule of the Misuse of Drugs Act and the Poisons Act schedules from Singapore Statutes Online, parse into a controlled-substance list with class, and record the statute version date.
12. **TGA ARTG** (Australia; downloadable; record terms). Active ingredient, ARTG id, status, and the SUSMP schedule number (AU control status, Schedules 2 through 9).
13. **EMA** (downloadable list of centrally authorised human medicines). Authorised, withdrawn, refused, with dates.
14. **MHRA products database and emc** (UK). Check terms first. If a bulk or API route exists under terms permitting reuse, ingest SmPC section 4.5 (interactions) and 5.1 (pharmacodynamics) and licence status; if not, record the scope in BLOCKERS and do not scrape. UK status stays "not cleared" honestly rather than dishonestly filled.
15. **PMDA** (Japan; English approved-products list). Cross-check DrugCentral's PMDA flag; record discrepancies.
16. **PharmGKB** (CC BY-SA 4.0) and **CPIC guidelines** (free). Clinical annotations by gene-drug pair with evidence level; CPIC dosing-guideline existence (existence and the gene involved, not the dosing itself). This feeds a pharmacogenomics section in Phase 4 that biohackers with genotype data will use heavily.
17. **ClinicalTrials.gov** (public domain). Already used; confirm `results_posted`, enrollment, phase, status, completion date are held for the trial-size and registry-gap sections.
18. **DDInter 2.0** (ddinter2.scbdd.com; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Retrieve into `data/validation/ddinter/` only. Never joined into rendered data, never in the release candidate, never in the corpus files. Its only permitted use is the Phase 4 validation comparison, and that comparison is gated on Felix confirming he is comfortable with non-commercial-licence data touching validation of a commercial site; until he does, validate against openFDA `drug_interactions` and the Inxight DDI dataset only. Record the gate in BLOCKERS.
19. Optional, run if time permits after 1–18 pass: **Open Targets** (target-disease evidence for hubs), **SureChEMBL** (structure-to-patent links for Tier 3).

Field census after: `scripts/revamp/field_census.py` → `data/revamp/field-census-after.csv` and `data/revamp/field-census-delta.csv`. Recompute `universal_blank`; any field that a source has now filled re-enters the denominator, and Phase 1's threshold script re-runs automatically (`derive_threshold.py` is idempotent and reads the current census).

**Gate G2**: every source in 1–18 has a status of `mapped`, `mapped-partial` with counts, or `BLOCKED-WITH-EVIDENCE`; LICENSES.md has an entry per source with all eight columns filled; delta census committed; UNII coverage on the 3,971 no-ChEMBL pages reported as a percentage; SG registration status populated on 100% of pages (as registered, not registered, or "not found in HSA listing as of <date>"); patent field populated for every US-approved small molecule with an Orange Book record.

## Phase 3 — Identity on a molecular spine
Subagent on Opus 5 (`identity-matcher`) for 3.1–3.3 and 3.6; lead on Fable 5.1 adjudicates 3.4.

3.1 Attach InChIKey and UNII to every page from Phase 2 outputs. Report coverage per tier.

3.2 Auto-resolution rules, applied in this order and logged per decision to `data/revamp/identity-decisions.csv` (page_a, page_b, rule, action, evidence):
- Identical UNII → merge, unless `docs/specs/identity-resolution.md` names an exception class.
- Identical full InChIKey → merge.
- Identical InChIKey skeleton, different full key → keep separate, link `form_of` both ways, and generate a one-line form note ("X is the mesylate salt of Y"; "X is the (S)-enantiomer of Y") from the structure difference. Enantiomer pairs with separate approvals stay separate pages.
- Same normalised name, different skeleton → different substances; resolve the display-name collision by appending a disambiguator drawn from the substance class or primary target, never by merging.
- Same name, no structure on either side → review list.

3.3 Run 3.2 against the 214 collisions, 697 suspected merges and 55 held conflicts. Record: auto-resolved count by rule, remaining count.

3.4 Second opinion on the remainder. A second `identity-matcher` instance receives the remaining pairs with a differently worded brief and without the first pass's verdicts, and returns merge / separate / form_of with evidence. Agreements are applied and logged. Disagreements go to `data/revamp/identity-review.md` with both verdicts, the evidence each cited, and a recommended default. The lead applies the default only where the identity spec's rules already license it; otherwise the pair is held unchanged and listed for Felix, ordered by reach (pairs touching indexable pages first, then the band, then the rest). State the estimated review time; it must be under two hours.

3.5 Redirect and orphan re-verification after every batch of merges or splits: all 870 legacy redirects answer 308; new slugs created by splits have redirects from any prior slug; zero orphans across the union of all legacy slugs. Script `scripts/revamp/redirect_check.py`, output `data/revamp/redirect-check.json`.

3.6 Rendered duplicate check, the one the identity spec requires and that never ran. `scripts/revamp/rendered_dup_check.py`: Playwright renders every indexable page and every hub plus a random 1,000-page sample of noindex pages, at 390 px and 1280 px; extracts visible text via `innerText` on the main content region, excluding nav, footer, search bar and supervision block; 5-word shingles, MinHash with 128 permutations; flags pairs at or above 0.5 Jaccard → `data/revamp/rendered-dups.csv`. For each flagged indexable pair: either the generator differentiates them (fix in Phase 4) or they are the same substance (merge in 3.2). Re-run until zero flagged pairs among indexable pages. This script becomes a required CI check in Phase 6.

3.7 Fix the 39 suppressed Tier 3 pages that state their classification in the header instead of the supervision block. Verify by rendering all 39.

**Gate G3**: identity-decisions.csv committed with counts by rule; identity-review.md under two hours of human work with reach ordering; redirect-check.json clean; rendered-dups.csv zero on the indexable set; 39 pages verified fixed.

## Phase 4 — Derived sections
Lead: Fable 5.1. This phase decides whether the site reads as expert work or as generated filler. Quality here is not delegable.

4.1 Interaction engine, three evidence tiers, stored as `interactions.parquet` (page_a, page_b, tier, direction, mechanism, source, source_date, rule_id, confidence):
- **Tier A, label-documented.** Parsed from openFDA `drug_interactions`, plus `contraindications` and `warnings_and_cautions` where an interaction is stated there. Store the sentence, the label `set_id` and `effective_time`. Entity-link the counterpart drug by name → UNII.
- **Tier B, curated.** Inxight DDI dataset rows. Store the Inxight record id.
- **Tier C, mechanism-predicted.** Rules over three inputs: (i) `cyp_profile`: a strong or moderate inhibitor or inducer of enzyme E paired with a substrate of E, with the label's sensitivity statement where present; (ii) shared primary target with same-direction action from ChEMBL/IUPHAR/DrugCentral; (iii) additive-effect classes derived from pharmacologic action: serotonergic, CNS-depressant, anticoagulant or antiplatelet, hypotensive, hypoglycaemic, nephrotoxic, hepatotoxic, hyperkalaemic, QT-prolonging where the label's `warnings_and_cautions` states QT prolongation. Each prediction records rule id, the exact inputs, the direction ("expected ↑ exposure of X", "additive Y effect"), and a confidence band (likely, possible).
- **Validation.** `scripts/revamp/validate_interactions.py` scores Tier C against Tier A as ground truth: precision and recall per rule, overall, and by confidence band → `data/revamp/interaction-validation.json`. Bars: predictions labelled "likely" must reach precision ≥ 0.60 overall; any rule under 0.40 precision is disabled and the reason recorded. If Felix has lifted the DDInter gate in BLOCKERS, run the same script against DDInter as a second reference and record both tables; DDInter data still never touches rendered output.
- **Rendering rules** (unit-tested in `tests/test_render_safety.py`): tier label visible on every interaction line; Tier C lines begin "Predicted from mechanism:" and show the derivation; where no interaction is found for a pair or a page, render "No interaction found in [list of sources checked] as of [date]"; the words "safe", "no interaction", "safe to combine" never appear in generated interaction text; controlled substances (any match against the SG MDA schedule, US DEA schedule, AU SUSMP Schedules 8–9, or UN conventions) render regulatory status, pharmacology, mechanism and interaction evidence, and the generator has no code path that emits dose, timing, route, or combination protocol text for them.

4.2 "Where it's registered" block on every page, one line per jurisdiction: Singapore (HSA listing status; forensic class POM/P/GSL; MOH subsidy if the list was ingested; MDA/Poisons schedule if matched), United States (Drugs@FDA or Orange Book status; Rx or OTC; DEA schedule), Australia (ARTG status; SUSMP schedule), United Kingdom (status if the source cleared, otherwise "UK register not cleared for this run"), European Union (EMA status), Japan (DrugCentral/PMDA flag). Each line carries status, source, and date checked. Unknowns render "Not found in [register] as of [date]". Combination products list each component. This block is the single most useful thing on the page for a Singapore reader; it must be on 100% of pages including Tier 3.

4.3 Generic and patent block from Orange Book and Purple Book: RLD, earliest unexpired patent expiry, exclusivity end, generic available yes/no with date of first generic approval, TE code. Pages with no Orange Book record render "No US patent or exclusivity data on record" and, where the reason is knowable (not an approved US small molecule; biologic with Purple Book data instead), say so.

4.4 Pharmacogenomics block from PharmGKB/CPIC: gene, level of evidence, phenotype affected (e.g. "CYP2C19 poor metabolisers: reduced activation"), and whether a CPIC guideline exists. Rendered only where a Level 1A–2B annotation exists. Never renders dose adjustments; it states that a guideline exists and links to it.

4.5 Tier 3 computed sections, rendered for humans; Tier 3 remains noindex. Each renders only when its inputs exist and never emits a placeholder.
- **Nearest approved structural neighbour.** RDKit Morgan fingerprints radius 2, 2048 bits; Tanimoto against all approved compounds; render the top match when similarity ≥ 0.40, with the similarity value and a description of the differing substituent from a maximum common substructure comparison, and the neighbour's status (approved, withdrawn, generic).
- **Potency rank.** Among compounds holding a pChEMBL value against the same primary target, this compound's rank and percentile, rendered when at least five comparators exist, with the assay type stated.
- **Activity timeline.** First and last publication year from ChEMBL document links; whether an approved drug has since been approved against the same target (target validated) or whether the last three trials on the target were terminated or withdrawn (target headwind); originating organisation from ChEMBL/Inxight where held, with a note if the organisation no longer appears in any post-2020 record.
- **Form-of note** from Phase 3 where applicable.

4.6 Existing derived sections (failure autopsy, registry-to-publication gap, same-target lineage, trial-size ceiling, N-of-1 designability, and the eight others): re-run after Phases 2–3, re-apply the 40-page floor, record firing counts before and after, and confirm N-of-1 designability still never fires on a suppressed page.

4.7 Slop gate. `scripts/revamp/slop_draw.py` samples 60 rendered pages (20 per tier) and, for every sentence in a derived section, checks: (a) it traces to a stored field or computed value (each generator emits a provenance map sentence → field); (b) after masking drug, target and number tokens, the sentence template appears on no more than 0.5% of pages corpus-wide; (c) no sentence is a label naming a device. The lead reads the 60 pages for the logical-sequence test Felix specifies: consecutive claims must follow from each other. Every failure is fixed in the generator, then the draw is re-run with a fresh sample. The gate passes on two consecutive clean draws. All draws are committed to `data/revamp/slop-draws/`.

**Gate G4**: interaction-validation.json meets the bars; registration block on 100% of pages; patent block on all eligible pages; PGx block firing count recorded; Tier 3 sections firing counts recorded; existing sections re-verified; two consecutive clean slop draws; `tests/test_render_safety.py` passing in CI.

## Phase 5 — Hubs
Lead: Fable 5.1.

5.1 Hub types. Target hubs: every target with ≥ 5 linked compounds of which ≥ 1 is approved or ≥ 3 reached clinical phase. Mechanism-class hubs: every ATC level-4 or IUPHAR class with ≥ 5 members in the corpus. Pathway hubs: every pathway in the LONGEVITY model's pathway field with ≥ 5 linked compounds (expect mTOR, AMPK, sirtuins, NAD+ metabolism, senolytic targets, autophagy, GLP-1, and others the data surfaces). Slugs under `/h/`.

5.2 Each hub carries a comparison table over its members (approval status by jurisdiction including Singapore, generic availability, potency where held, approved indications, withdrawn flag with reason, trial count and results-posted share, Tier 3 count) and a written synthesis generated from that table and the members' derived sections: what is approved, what died and why (from the failure autopsies), what a Singapore reader can actually access under which forensic class, what is predicted from mechanism, and what the registry-to-publication gap says about the target. The synthesis passes the Phase 4 slop gate.

5.3 Indexing. Hubs are indexable and in the sitemap. Leaves below the per-tier threshold are `noindex,follow` and link to every hub they belong to. Every hub links to every leaf. `scripts/revamp/link_graph_check.py` verifies: no hub with fewer than 5 leaves; no indexable leaf without at least one hub; no hub unreachable from the site navigation.

5.4 First batch: the 20 target hubs and 10 pathway hubs with the highest (approved-drug count × longevity relevance, where relevance = 1 for pathway hubs and for targets carrying an ITP or ageing-trial link, else 0.5). Measure positional and lexical overlap across the batch with the corpus-20k scripts; both must clear 0.20 and 0.353. Then generate all hubs meeting the 5.1 criteria and re-measure.

**Gate G5**: ≥ 30 hubs live and measured under the lines; full hub set generated and measured; link-graph check clean; hubs in sitemap.
