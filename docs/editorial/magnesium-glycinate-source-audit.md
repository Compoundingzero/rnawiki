# Magnesium glycinate: source and claim audit

Status: independent evidence review for a preview, not a professional clinical sign-off or a recommendation to use a supplement. This file is internal; the reader page should show the finding and its limits, not the review workflow.

## Exact question

Does magnesium **bisglycinate** improve sleep in generally healthy adults who report poor sleep? Do not transfer an answer about this form to magnesium lysinate glycinate, other magnesium salts, or people with untreated chronic disease.

## Follow-up checks for the formerly empty reader sections

- **Who was missing:** [DRKS00031494](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00031494) excluded severe renal or liver disease, cancer, people receiving treatment for sleep disorder or depression, and major schedule changes such as shift work. Entry age was 18–65. These are boundaries of this trial, not proof that no research exists for those groups.
- **What was measured:** [Schuster et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12412596/) used the seven-question Insomnia Severity Index as its main outcome. Fewer than 10% completed enough voluntary diary entries for meaningful sleep-time analysis. Other between-group sleep and daytime questionnaire comparisons were not clearly different. The apparent larger response at lower reported dietary magnesium intake was exploratory and used a nonvalidated single-question intake measure, with no biochemical magnesium-status test.
- **How the newer synthesis changes the reading:** The [2026 randomized-trial review](https://doi.org/10.1080/19390211.2026.2719670) found inconsistent results across magnesium formulations and outcomes and rated certainty low or very low. Its conclusion applies to oral magnesium across forms; it does not provide a new bisglycinate-specific effect estimate. The 2025 trial remains the direct evidence for this exact form, with its small and uncertain score difference. The review could not establish that low baseline magnesium intake identifies responders or that a particular form or duration works best.
- **What went wrong in the trial:** Table 5 reports two adverse events among 77 magnesium recipients and seven among 76 placebo recipients, with no serious event reported. This four-week selected sample cannot establish comparative long-term or rare-event safety. The [NIH fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/) remains the magnesium-wide source for digestive harms, kidney impairment, and medicine interactions.
- **Why people stopped, and a source inconsistency:** The [paper's participant-flow text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12412596/) says 69/77 assigned magnesium and 65/78 assigned placebo completed the four weeks. It attributes two placebo withdrawals to stomach pain and leaves 19 withdrawal reasons unspecified. Its adverse-event table note instead says nobody discontinued because of adverse events. Do not present a neat discontinuation rate or reason breakdown without resolving that internal inconsistency with the authors. The main adverse-event table uses 77 magnesium and 76 placebo participants, which is a different denominator from the randomized group sizes.
- **How it is supplied and checked:** [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/information-consumers-using-dietary-supplements/questions-and-answers-dietary-supplements) says U.S. supplements are not approved before marketing, while makers must follow manufacturing and labeling rules and FDA can enforce against unsafe or misbranded products. A [USP quality-verification mark](https://www.usp.org/sites/default/files/usp/document/about/convention-membership/usp-ds-qual-pharmacists-fact-sheet.pdf) can assess a specific product's identity and quality, not its sleep efficacy.
- **Why the trial timeline matters:** [DRKS00031494](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00031494) was registered 12 April 2023, before recruitment began 20 April, and records completion 14 July 2023. Its primary insomnia-score outcome preceded the published result. A registration alone is not an efficacy finding.
- **Funding and interests:** The [paper's funding statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12412596/) says an institute at Leibniz University Hannover funded the study. Its disclosures say one author leads a contract research company funded by nutraceutical companies and has received industry honoraria. This is a consideration for independent replication, not a reason to discard or invert the result.

## Claims deliberately not made

- No claim that the 1.6-point group-average difference is a clinically important improvement for an individual.
- No claim of longer sleep, improved recovery, better daytime performance, or benefit beyond four weeks.
- No claim that glycinate is generally better absorbed than oxide, or that results transfer to lysinate glycinate.
- No dosing protocol, self-experiment instructions, or advice to replace care for insomnia.
- No claim that a magnesium-wide review is a replicated bisglycinate trial, or that its overall conclusion is a form-specific treatment verdict.
- No claim that the site has a published use-specific conclusion for magnesium glycinate.

## Source-check rule for other dossiers

For each proposed public sentence, record the exact substance/form, use, people, comparator, outcome, time, source URL, and what it **does not** establish. If one field is missing, write an honest unavailable state and keep the section short; do not generate a generic caution card to fill the space. This audit is not evidence that any other dossier has been checked.
