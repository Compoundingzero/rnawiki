# An all-drug evidence checkpoint, built from exact sources

19 September 2026. This is a research and copy draft, not a production publication. The unit of work is **a specific question about a specific substance, form, and population**, not a generic drug paragraph. A label statement describes one labelled product; a trial result describes one study. Neither automatically answers what a different product or a healthy reader will experience.

## What is actually broken

The current `/d` inventory has 10,250 slugs, but those are not 10,250 equally researched medicines. Exact-slug reconciliation finds 9,855 background envelopes and 395 inventory pages without one. The 28,832-row tier corpus is a different denominator: only 9,360 of the inventory slugs appear there. These denominators must not be blended into a progress percentage. Run `node --import tsx scripts/research/coverage-queue.ts --limit 0` for the counts and scope.

The apparent abundance of human-study cards is misleading. A **stale 12 September inventory artifact** counts 6,513 pages with such cards and records **zero reviewed claims**; it is not a current rendered-page count. The old card constructor starts from a trial identifier and endpoint, then fills condition, participants, form, comparator, duration, and result with generic “not recorded” text. A ClinicalTrials.gov URL alone does not establish the missing fields. The separate background corpus has 22 stored result rows over 18 records; 19 cite 16 distinct ClinicalTrials.gov studies and three cite FDA labels. The live registry candidate pass fetched all 16 NCTs, but 14 of 19 rows have at least one matching blocker. This is a source-binding problem, not a lack of prose. See [the card-constructor audit](human-result-card-audit.md).

The product-label corpus is more promising for product-specific facts: its 124,706 mapped rows include 1,872 pages with contraindication sections, 1,609 with population sections, 1,351 with overdose sections, and 726 with boxed-warning sections. However, the mapped row does not retain a dependable product/form/route pairing. A match by ingredient identifier can reach combination products. It must not be rendered as an ingredient-wide assertion. [FDA's label field reference](https://open.fda.gov/fields/druglabel_reference.pdf) distinguishes the stable SPL `set_id` from version-specific product content; product identity needs an exact-set-ID join to current label metadata.

## Research lanes, not templates pretending to know

| Lane                            | First source and join key                                                                                      | Reader answer produced                                                     | When to stop and investigate                                                                            |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity                        | Exact substance identifier and product composition; distinguish ingredient, salt, combination, branded product | “What exactly is this page about?”                                         | Shared name, chemical mistaken for medicine, conflicting or absent ingredient identity                  |
| Product facts                   | One current US SPL `set_id` and version; exact product name, form, route, section and excerpt                  | “For this labelled product, what is it used for and what warnings matter?” | Set ID changed, several routes/forms, ingredient-only or combination match, or section not applicable   |
| Human outcome                   | Exact NCT and posted outcome module, checked against its primary publication when available                    | “Who was studied, against what, what changed, and when?”                   | Registered-only study, ambiguous arm/outcome, missing denominator, conflicting source figures           |
| Other jurisdictions/supplements | Country-specific regulator record or product document, plus direct human study if a benefit is claimed         | “What is sold here and what evidence fits that form?”                      | Importing a US label into another jurisdiction or treating an ingredient fact as a product-quality test |
| Evidence gap                    | Recorded search date, sources searched, exact question and missing field                                       | “What evidence would change this answer?”                                  | The search boundary is unspecified or “not found” is written as “does not exist”                        |

The queue is deterministic and read-only: `scripts/research/coverage-queue.ts` assigns 956 identity cases, 1,625 product-label-safety cases, 4,540 human-result-scope cases, 65 background-acquisition cases, 2,632 thin-record cases, and 432 baseline-claim checks. These six lanes account for **all 10,250 slugs**, but none means “ready.” Within each lane, prioritize likely reader demand and potential harm, then batch by source family (same SPL/registry API) to avoid researching each slug from scratch. All 10,250 slugs must be classified as a real drug, a distinct non-drug substance, or an identity problem before any site-wide promise is defensible.

The 19-row result queue is only a starting slice. A separate September 2026 ClinicalTrials.gov mapping contains 69,304 aggregate rows for 8,663 pages in the larger corpus, linking 155,645 distinct studies. **Every link is a normalized intervention-name candidate**, not a confirmed product/study match; 239,036 page–study pairs are shared by more than one page. The mapped data lists which NCTs post results, but not the actual outcome table. The new `scripts/research/trial-discovery-queue.py --slug dapagliflozin --limit 3` demonstrates the scale: 323 name-matched studies and 116 with posted-results flags. Its exact page-key display name is _dapagliflozin propanediol_, so even this apparently obvious slug needs an identity/form check before a result is attached. For scale, use the map only to discover NCTs, then fetch exact study result records and apply the same intervention-role, form, condition, population, arm, outcome, time and denominator checks as the 19-row pilot. Never turn `hasResults=true` into a benefit statement. [ClinicalTrials.gov mapping audit](../../data/sources/clinicaltrials/coverage.json).

## The reader contract

Each visible claim should fit one card, with technical detail behind disclosure:

1. **Question:** one outcome the reader understands, such as sleep quality or heart-failure hospitalization—not a medicine-wide “works” verdict.
2. **Who and what:** studied group, exact form/route, comparison and follow-up. If one is unknown, state the exact missing field once, where it affects interpretation.
3. **What happened:** both groups' actual numbers, unit, timepoint and uncertainty. Show absolute numbers where the source supports them; do not substitute a percentage or biomarker for lived benefit.
4. **Boundary:** the smallest limitation that changes the reading, such as excluded patients, a composite endpoint, short follow-up or a different formulation.
5. **Source:** direct original document and precise table/section/field path, with source date/version. Each factual sentence has a citation, not merely a “Sources” accordion on a large card.

Do not repeat “RNAWiki has not published a reviewed conclusion” in every section. That is site process metadata, not the missing answer. No automated step should invent a contraindication, route, trial population or effect estimate; when source text is missing, the queue should request the missing source rather than emit a generic filler sentence. A trial may have no meaningful human outcome, and a substance may not be a medicine. That distinction is useful information when supported by the search record.

### Replace the photographed filler at its source

| Existing reader question                  | Substance-specific research answer and compact visual                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “What it does” / “path through the body”  | A source-supported **form → route → absorption or distribution → relevant target → measured outcome** path. Stop the diagram at the last established step; do not draw a therapeutic outcome from a mechanism alone.                          |
| “What happened in people”                 | A two-arm result panel: **who/condition**, exact tested product and comparison, **both numbers**, unit, follow-up and interval. Link the selected source table, not just the study homepage.                                                  |
| “Why it might seem to do nothing”         | Start with the actual measured outcome and size of effect. Name a population/form/time mismatch only when the study or label establishes it; do not generate hypothetical adherence or product-quality explanations as facts about this drug. |
| “How supplied / form / pack / registered” | One exact product-label identity strip: brand/generic, ingredient(s), strength, form, route, country, SPL set ID or application. Separate **listed product** from **approved use** and avoid generic “varies by country” cards.               |
| “What could I measure?”                   | Explain the endpoint actually used in the best-matched study and whether it maps to something a person would notice. Do not turn a study duration into an instruction for a home experiment.                                                  |
| “What nobody knows”                       | One concrete untested transfer: e.g., the DAPA-HF population does not answer a healthy-person performance claim. Point to the exact excluded group, unmeasured endpoint, or follow-up limit.                                                  |
| Glossary / QA / “what next”               | Define a term only beside the real number that uses it. Keep QA gates off the reader page; replace generic next-step prompts with a sourced, unanswered claim question or omit the link.                                                      |

The paired-arm and body-path diagrams are reusable SVG/CSS components, but their nodes and labels are data, not a universal illustration. A blank node never becomes a sentence of filler.

## Worked study draft: dapagliflozin and heart failure

**Question.** In adults with symptomatic heart failure and reduced pumping function, did oral dapagliflozin change the chance of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure compared with placebo?

**Who was studied.** Adults with symptomatic heart failure and a left-ventricular ejection fraction of 40% or less. They received background heart-failure care. The [NCT03036124 eligibility record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03036124) specifies these conditions and excludes, among others, type 1 diabetes and severe kidney disease at entry. This is not a study of healthy people seeking performance or longevity.

**What was compared and measured.** The registered active arm used oral dapagliflozin tablets; the primary posted result identifies the analysed arm as 10 mg tablets versus matching placebo. The outcome was a **combined event**: cardiovascular death, hospitalization for heart failure, or an urgent heart-failure visit. It was not a standalone test of exercise ability or lifespan. The registry's result time frame extends up to 27.8 months; the [primary DAPA-HF paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31535829/) reports median follow-up of 18.2 months. Those are different time descriptions, not a contradiction.

**What happened.** The primary event occurred in **386 of 2,373 (16.3%)** in the dapagliflozin group and **502 of 2,371 (21.2%)** in the placebo group, a difference of **4.9 percentage points** in observed proportions. The [paper's abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31535829/) reports a hazard ratio of **0.74 (95% CI 0.65–0.85)** for the time-to-first combined event. The counts and analysis are independently cross-checked against the [posted registry outcome](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03036124?tab=results), `resultsSection.outcomeMeasuresModule.outcomeMeasures[0]`. The hazard ratio is not “26% more life,” and the 4.9-point difference should not be carried into a different population or follow-up period.

**What remains outside this answer.** This study does not establish a benefit for a healthy biohacker, for a different outcome, or for every dapagliflozin-containing product. Safety and suitability need a separate current-product-label check. This draft describes a trial result; it does not recommend starting, stopping or changing a medicine.

### First-screen candidate

> **Dapagliflozin, heart failure with reduced pumping function:** In DAPA-HF, about 16 in 100 taking dapagliflozin and 21 in 100 taking placebo had cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure during the study. The trial studied adults already receiving heart-failure care, not healthy people. [Study and results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03036124?tab=results) · [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31535829/)

The first screen uses two short sentences and exposes the source. A compact 100-person paired bar can show **16 vs 21**, labelled “combined outcome,” while the disclosure explains actual denominators, follow-up and uncertainty. The visual is a scale translation of observed proportions, not a predicted personal benefit.

A supplement needs a different path. The existing [magnesium-bisglycinate sleep source audit](../editorial/magnesium-glycinate-source-audit.md) already ties a four-week human sleep-score result to the tested bisglycinate form and refuses to transfer it to every magnesium salt or product. That is a third worked pattern; FDA prescription-label automation cannot replace it.

## Repeatable production mechanics

1. Read the coverage queue to select an exact slug and lane. Resolve identity before benefit extraction.
2. Use `scripts/research/label-candidates.py` for bounded, source-verbatim SPL candidate passages; `scripts/research/trial-discovery-queue.py` for name-linked NCT leads across the broader corpus; and `scripts/research/trial-candidate-queue.ts` for a fully contextual exact-NCT result candidate in the 19-row pilot. None of these outputs is page copy.
3. Build a `ResearchDraft` (`lib/research/claim-draft.ts`) with short reader sentences, one exact source excerpt/locator per field and explicit `this_product_only` or `this_study_only` scope. `assessResearchDraft` catches missing fields, cross-record joins, unsupported quotes and overlong first-read copy; its passing result is not a medical truth verdict.
4. Independently compare numeric results with the original publication or regulator assessment, including denominators, outcome definition, analysis population and whether the comparison is randomized. Reconcile discrepancies before writing numbers. Record why a source was chosen or rejected.
5. Render only the fields that answer this particular question. The page can still expose mechanism, body path, harms and alternatives, but each item needs its own source and scope. No “evidence may be wrong” or “recorded by the evidence model” card is an acceptable substitute.
6. Re-run source freshness and page tests. Promote in batches with a measurable numerator: classified identities, exact product labels, use-specific human results, and source-bound first screens. A homepage promise changes only when the visible coverage actually supports it.

**Release criterion for an all-drug claim:** every public `/d` slug has a resolved entity type and at least one first-screen answer tied to the exact product or use-specific study, or a dated, explicitly bounded search showing which requested outcome remains unestablished. Every numeric benefit shown has both arms/denominators, outcome definition, time and source; every product warning names the exact product scope. Run the mobile/desktop source-link and navigation tests on a stratified sample of medicine, supplement, combination, withdrawn and identity-ambiguous pages. The current inventory and research queues do **not** meet that criterion; a universal homepage headline would still over-promise.

The sources used by the pipeline are [openFDA drug-label records](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/label/how-to-use-the-endpoint/) and the [ClinicalTrials.gov study data structure](https://clinicaltrials.gov/data-api/about-api/study-data-structure); where neither answers a claim, search original publications via [NCBI E-utilities](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK25501/) and record the search boundary. An API hit is a research lead, not an answer by itself.
